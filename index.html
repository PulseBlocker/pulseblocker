PulseBlocker Privacy Policy

Effective 23 September 2026 · Applies to the PulseBlocker app for Android

PulseBlocker helps you block distracting apps and websites, run focus sessions and understand how you use your phone. This policy explains what data the app handles, where it goes, and the choices you have. In this policy, "PulseBlocker", "we" and "us" mean the developer of the PulseBlocker app. You can reach us at pulseblocker@gmail.com.

The short version
  • Almost everything PulseBlocker does happens on your phone and stays there.
  • An account is optional. Without one, none of your data is sent to our server.
  • With an account, your profile, focus history, daily usage summary and settings are synced to our server so they follow you between devices.
  • We show no ads, use no advertising ID or analytics trackers, and never sell your data.
  • You can delete your account and its data at any time from inside the app.

1. Data that stays on your phone

To block apps and track your focus, PulseBlocker needs to see things about how you use your phone. The following is processed on your device only. It is not sent to us or to anyone else, except where section 3 or 4 says otherwise.

App usage (Usage Access)

Which apps you used and for how long, to show your screen time, enforce usage limits and decide when to block. If you have an account, a daily summary is synced (section 3).

Screen content (Accessibility Service)

PulseBlocker uses Android's Accessibility Service to do its core job: to know which app or website is in front of you so it can block it. With this permission the app reads which app is open, the address shown in your browser's address bar, whether a short-video feed (such as YouTube Shorts, Instagram Reels, TikTok or Facebook Reels) is on screen, and scrolling activity for scroll limits. It also uses this permission to block split-screen and floating windows when you ask it to. This information is used the moment it is read and is not stored or sent anywhere. PulseBlocker does not use the Accessibility Service to read what you type, your passwords or the content of your messages.

Screenshots for the optional image shield

If you turn on the AI image shield in Adult Block, the app briefly captures what is on screen and checks it for explicit imagery with a model that runs entirely on your phone. The capture is analysed in memory and discarded straight away. It is never saved and never leaves your device.

Notifications

If you choose to block notifications, PulseBlocker reads notifications as they arrive in order to silence the ones you've chosen. The app name, title and text of silenced notifications are kept on your phone so you can review them later in the digest. They are never uploaded. Only a daily count of silenced notifications is synced if you have an account.

Location and Wi-Fi

If you set up a location trigger, the places you choose are stored on your phone. Android tells the app when you arrive at or leave them, including in the background. If you set up a Wi-Fi trigger, the app reads the name of the Wi-Fi network you're connected to. Neither your location nor your Wi-Fi network names are sent to our server. When you choose a place, the app may ask Google's services to show a map and to look up the place's name (section 4).

Everything else you set up

Your blocklists, blocked websites, schedules, templates, Study Mode profiles, block history and the list of apps installed on your phone (so you can pick which to block) are stored on your phone.

Microphone

PulseBlocker uses the microphone only when you choose to record a voice note to attach to a problem report. It never listens at any other time.

2. Using PulseBlocker without an account

Every feature works without an account. As a guest, the data described in sections 1 and 3 stays on your phone. Only the services in section 4 are contacted, and only when you use the features that need them.

3. If you create an account

Creating an account is optional. It lets you keep your profile and focus history across devices.

Account information

  • Email address, to identify your account and to send you account emails (such as a sign-up confirmation or a password reset link).
  • Password, if you sign up with email. It is handled by our authentication system and stored only as a secure hash. We never see it.
  • Google or Microsoft sign-in. If you sign in with Google or Microsoft, we receive your email address and an account identifier from that provider.
  • Username and the avatar you pick from our preset illustrations. Usernames must be unique, so other signed-in users can check whether a username is already taken.

Data synced to your account

  • Focus subjects: their names and colours.
  • Focus and break sessions: the subject or break type, how long the session lasted and when it ended.
  • A daily usage summary: total screen time, time spent scrolling, number of phone pickups, longest continuous use, longest focus session and the number of notifications silenced.
  • Per-app usage for the day: for up to 40 of the apps you used most that day, the app's name and package name, minutes used and whether you marked it as distracting or productive.
  • Your PulseBlocker settings, such as your focus goal, timer and scroll-limit settings. They include the apps you allowed or blocked from sending notifications and the apps allowed during Study Mode.
  • Your phone's manufacturer and model, so you can tell which device last saved your settings.

This data is stored on our server (a Supabase database at api.pulsepom.io). Access to it is restricted so that your account can read and change only its own data. We use it only to provide the sync and history features. We don't use it for advertising or share it with anyone else.

About the DNS protection VPN

When you turn on DNS protection, Android asks you to approve a VPN connection. PulseBlocker uses it only to send your phone's DNS lookups (the step that turns a website name into an address) to the filtering provider you chose, which declines to look up adult sites. Only DNS lookups pass through it. The rest of your internet traffic doesn't go through the VPN and isn't seen or inspected by PulseBlocker. We never receive your DNS lookups.

5. Problem reports and crash reports

Problem reports. When you send a report from the app's Support page, we receive:

  • what you write;
  • any photos, videos or voice notes you choose to attach;
  • your phone's model and Android version;
  • if you are signed in, your account ID, email address and username, so we can reply to you.

Guests' reports carry no account information.

Crash reports. If the app crashes, a technical report is sent automatically. It contains the error, the app version and your phone's model and Android version. We set Sentry not to record your IP address.

Both are handled by Sentry, used only to fix problems and improve the app, and kept for no longer than 90 days.

7. What we don't do

  • We don't show ads or use your data for advertising.
  • We don't sell or rent your data, or share it with data brokers.
  • We don't use the advertising ID, analytics SDKs or cross-app tracking.

8. How long we keep data

  • Account and synced data: until you delete your account.
  • Problem reports and crash reports: up to 90 days.
  • Data on your phone: until you delete it in the app, clear the app's data or uninstall PulseBlocker.

9. Deleting your account and data

In the app: on the Home screen, tap your profile picture in the top-right corner to open Account, then tap Delete account. This permanently deletes your account and all the data synced to it: your profile, focus subjects and sessions, daily usage, per-app usage and settings. It takes effect immediately. Data stored only on your phone is not touched, and the app keeps working without an account. To remove that too, clear the app's data or uninstall it.

Without the app: if you no longer have the app installed, follow the steps on our account deletion page.

10. Security

All communication between the app and our server uses encrypted HTTPS connections. Our database enforces that each account can reach only its own data. Your sign-in tokens are stored in the app's private storage on your phone. No system is perfectly secure, but we work to protect your data and keep what we collect to a minimum.

11. Children

PulseBlocker is not directed at children under 13, and we don't knowingly collect personal data from them. If you believe a child under 13 has created an account, contact us and we'll delete it.

12. Your rights

Depending on where you live, you may have the right to access, correct, delete or receive a copy of your personal data, or to object to how we use it. You can delete your account in the app at any time. For anything else, email pulseblocker@gmail.com. We'll respond within 30 days.

We process account data to provide the service you signed up for, and crash and problem reports under our legitimate interest in keeping the app working. We rely on your consent for the optional features that need special permissions, which you can withdraw at any time by turning the feature off or revoking the permission in Android settings. Our server and service providers may be located outside your country.

13. Changes to this policy

If we change this policy, we'll update the date at the top of this page. If a change significantly affects how your data is handled, we'll also let you know in the app.
